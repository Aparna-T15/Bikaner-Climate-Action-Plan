A Decadal Analysis of Temperature Extremes and Rainfall Variability in Bikaner, India
A geospatial climate-analysis report examining heat-wave patterns, maximum-temperature changes, and rainfall variability in Bikaner District, Rajasthan, India.

Study period: Temperature and heat-wave analysis: 2014–2024; rainfall analysis: 2015–2024, with a national rainfall comparison for 2020–2024.
Report year: 2025
Prepared by: Aparna
Submitted to: BCAP (Bikaner Climate Action Plan)

Overview
This report explores how extreme heat and rainfall patterns have varied across Bikaner District. It combines annual and monthly heat-wave summaries, spatial comparisons of average maximum temperature, a temperature-change map, and a comparison of district rainfall with all-India annual rainfall figures.

The purpose is to provide a concise evidence base for understanding local climate stress and informing heat-action and water-management planning.

Study components
1. Heat waves and temperature extremes (2014–2024)
The report examines daily maximum-temperature data for March–September and summarizes days meeting the temperature thresholds used in the report:

Heat wave: actual maximum temperature of at least 45 °C.
Severe heat wave: actual maximum temperature of at least 47 °C.
It compares annual counts, monthly occurrence, and spatial patterns in average maximum temperature, including maps comparing 2014 and 2024 and a temperature-change map for 2014–2024.

2. Rainfall variability
The rainfall section presents patterns for 2015–2024 and compares Bikaner District rainfall with all-India annual rainfall figures for 2020–2024.

The report gives a district rainfall range of approximately 310.59–536.31 mm annually for the mapped period and uses approximately 424 mm as a mid-range district value in its comparison.

Key findings reported
2024 had the highest annual count of heat-wave and severe-heat-wave days in the report, with 30 days.
May and June were the main months for heat-wave activity; the report notes substantial activity in May in 2022 and 2024.
The mapped minimum of monthly average maximum temperature increased from 30.67 °C in 2014 to 31.38 °C in 2024; the mapped maximum changed from 43.04 °C to 43.09 °C.
The report identifies areas including Khajuwala, Nokha, Poogal, and Chhattargarh as locations with very high mapped temperature increases.
Bikaner District rainfall was substantially lower than the all-India annual rainfall values cited in the report, highlighting the district's dry-climate and water-stress context.
These are findings as presented in the report and should be interpreted in light of the data, spatial methods, and assumptions documented there.

Data sources described in the report
Temperature: NASA POWER Access Viewer daily maximum-temperature data for March–September, 2014–2024.
National rainfall comparison: India Meteorological Department (IMD) annual rainfall statistics cited in the report.
